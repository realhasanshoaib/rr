Place first, then teach

WP 1 · ORIENT

Every screen opens with its coordinate, its section on the rail, and now its position inside the slide. A lost reader spends working memory on "where am I?" — Traverse spends design so that question never comes up.

One step at full contrast

WP 2 · FOCUS

A slide never competes with itself. The current step owns the ink; absorbed steps drop to secondary ink but stay legible for reference. The pause between key presses is thinking time the design protects.

Recall is first-class

WP 3 · RETRIEVE

Reading feels like knowing — it isn't. Sections close with recall cards that ask before they tell, because the check is what writes the memory.

Two voices, two laws

The reading voice is Avenir Next; the instrument voice is SF Mono — numbers, paths, coordinates and labels only. And the scale now obeys two laws written for focus: a column always opens with its largest line, and nothing on a slide is allowed to whisper.

The descending column

Law · 01

A column opens with its largest line and sizes only fall from there. Kind-labels ride the title line; coordinates live in the chrome. Nothing small ever sits above something big.

The floor

Law · 02

Content never sets below 16px; chrome never below 13px. Hierarchy is carried by weight, ink against muted, sans against mono — never by shrinking.

Two new instruments

Same machining as Waypoint: 24-unit grid, 2px stroke, square caps, miter joins, no fills, currentColor throughout. The merge adds exactly two symbols, drawn in the same hand — flip for recall cards and substep for the subdivided rail.

The build already tree-shakes the sprite; a document that flips no cards ships no flip icon.

DO

2px, squared, stroke-only. The new glyphs are indistinguishable in manner from the original eight.

DON'T

No rounded caps, no hairline strokes, no fills. Beam's bubbly icon voice stayed in Beam.

One build, five waypoints, your pace

A real lesson, set in Traverse: what happens when you run node build.mjs doc.rr.html — → reveals each waypoint. The route below shows every coordinate now; the content is earned. Watch the rail: the Specimen tick has subdivided.

  1. Read the source, find every <rr-*> tag

    WP·1 — PARSE

    The rr-doc shell is located first; everything else must live inside it.

  2. Match each tag against its template in components/

    WP·2 — EXPAND

    Props become attributes, children drop into slots, defaults fill in.

  3. Fail loudly on anything unknown

    WP·3 — VERIFY

    Unknown component, missing prop, unregistered icon — the build stops and names the offender. Errors surface at build time, never in front of the reader.

  4. Copy only the icons actually used out of icons.svg

    WP·4 — TREE-SHAKE

    A document that uses 10 symbols ships 10 symbols, not the library.

  5. Write one self-contained HTML file

    WP·5 — EMIT

Ghost waypoints

The parents disagreed: Beam hid what's coming so you couldn't read ahead; Waypoint showed everything so you always knew the terrain. The synthesis — a ghost waypoint shows the shape of what's coming (its coordinate, its reserved space) and holds back the content until you ask. You preview the route without spoiling the pace.

WP 4 · EMIT

GHOST — coordinate visible, content withheld, space fully reserved. Nothing moves when it arrives.

WP 3 · VERIFY

CURRENT — full ink, survey-blue coordinate. The only thing the eye is asked to hold.

WP 2 · EXPAND

ABSORBED — dropped to secondary ink. Still legible for reference, no longer loud.

ON THE RAIL — the current tick subdivides, one dash per step; the wide dash is where you stand inside the slide.

Why it matters for learningSpine × Beam
Orientation and pacing stop being separate systems. One glance at the rail answers both questions — which section am I in, and how far through this idea am I? — and the pause before each reveal is where retrieval happens. State changes are contrast changes: nothing moves, nothing slides in, ≤150ms, pure swaps under reduced motion.
